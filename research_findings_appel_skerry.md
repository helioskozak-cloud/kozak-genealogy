## Batch 1 — William Appel + Susan

### The breakthrough: the middle names were the map

The Appel family carries surnames as middle names, consistently, across
generations. Reading the children's full names as data rather than as names is
what broke this open in the first hour:

| Full name | The surname hidden in it | Whose it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Edmund Skerry Appel** | **Skerry** | his mother's maiden name |
| **William Herr Appel Jr.** | **Herr** | his father's middle name — a further line back |
| **Collin Appel Kozak** | Appel | his mother's maiden name (next generation down) |

That pattern predicted that Susan's "S." was a surname, and it was.

### S125 — Jane (Skerry) Shumway, obituary — **CONFIRMED**

Susan's elder sister. Sisters' obituaries name the same parents, and this one
gives the father's name in full where Susan's gave only an initial.

**Evidence Tier: Tier 2** (newspaper obituary)
**Source:** Brattleboro Reformer (VT), Sep 2007 — `reformer.com`

**Jane (Skerry) Shumway** — b. **Oct 24 1921, Brattleboro, Vermont**;
d. **Sep 4 2007, Cheshire Medical Center, Keene NH**, aged 85.

- **Daughter of Clarence E. and Marion Anna (Cook) Skerry.**
- Brattleboro High School, class of 1940; became a registered nurse — the same
  school and profession as Susan.
- m. **William E. Shumway, Dec 31 1947, Brattleboro VT**.
- **Burial: Hillcrest Cemetery, Proctorsville, Vermont.**

**Confidence: Confirmed.**

**Two things this settles:**

1. **The "C." in C. Edmund Skerry is CLARENCE.** He is **Clarence Edmund
   Skerry** — Susan's obituary gave the initial, Jane's gives the name.
2. **Proctorsville is a village in the town of Cavendish, Vermont** — which is
   exactly where Susan's memorial service was held 11 years later. **Hillcrest
   Cemetery, Proctorsville is the family burial ground**, and is now the single
   most promising target for the whole Skerry line: Clarence and Marion are
   likely buried there, and cemetery plots cluster by family.

**Also worth noting:** the mother is **Marion *Anna* Cook**, and Susan's
daughter is **Elizabeth *Anna* Appel**. The Anna carries down three
generations — consistent with the naming pattern that broke this open.

### S126 — The Skerry surname: origin and distribution — **SUPPORTS THE LORE**

**Evidence Tier: Tier 3** (commercial surname-origin reference; the 1911 figure
is census-derived). Treat as orientation, not proof of this family's descent.

Two facts that bear directly on the family story:

- **Skerry is an Irish surname from GALWAY** — recorded as a variant of Scarry
  or Scurry.
- **In the 1911 Canadian census there were 34 Skerry families in Nova Scotia —
  about 51% of all Skerrys in Canada**, the highest concentration in the
  country.

**This is the lore, independently and from the surname side alone: Galway →
Nova Scotia.** Neither fact was known to whoever passed the story down, and
neither was reachable from anything already in this project.

**But be precise about what it does and does not show.** It establishes that
*Skerrys as a population* fit the Galway→Nova Scotia corridor. It does **not**
yet show that **our** Skerrys — Clarence Edmund Skerry's line in Vermont —
descend from those Nova Scotia Skerrys. That link is unproven and is the
central remaining question of the project.

The lore named four surnames. It now looks likely that **only one of them,
Skerry, carries the Galway/Nova Scotia story**, and that Cook, Appel and
Spalding were swept into it by association.

## What this complicates — read before Batch 6

**The geography is Vermont, not Ireland.** Canaan VT, Brattleboro VT, Middlesex
VT, Cavendish VT. Nothing in this obituary mentions Ireland, Galway, Nova
Scotia or Canada.

That does **not** disprove the lore — Susan was born in 1926, five or six
generations downstream of any 19th-century crossing, and obituaries rarely
reach back that far. But it does relocate the question. Two things now matter:

1. **Canaan, Vermont sits directly on the Quebec border**, at the top of the
   Connecticut River valley. If a family came south out of Atlantic Canada into
   northern New England, this is exactly the sort of place they would land. The
   corridor is geographically live, just displaced from where the lore put it.
2. **Spalding is still unplaced** — it appears nowhere in this obituary. The
   likeliest remaining slots are Marion Cook's mother, or the Shumway/Mitchell
   marriages, or the Appel/Herr side.

**Revised working hypothesis for the corridor:**
`Galway → Nova Scotia → northern Vermont → southern VT/NH → CT/ME/KY/GA`,
with **Skerry** as the surname most likely to carry it.

## Data-quality note on an existing source

**S123 cites a "CT Death Index API (data.ct.gov)".** Checked 2026-08-24: the
Socrata catalog for data.ct.gov no longer exposes any person-level death index.
`ie5j-mz6w` ("Connecticut Resident Deaths, 1999-2013") is **aggregate counts by
year/cause/age band**, not individual records, and there is no other candidate
dataset. Either that dataset was withdrawn, or S123's citation is wrong about
its source. **S123's underlying claim should be re-verified from another
source before it is relied on again.**
